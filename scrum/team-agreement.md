## 1. Team Values

Our team values respect, accountability, reliability, inclusion, and fairness.

We will listen to each other, complete the work we agree to take on, and communicate honestly about our progress and difficulties. We will support members who are learning unfamiliar skills and distribute work fairly based on effort, complexity, and availability.

## 2. Meetings

- **Frequency:** At least once per week for approximately 45–60 minutes.
- **Regular meeting:** Tuesday lab, with an additional meeting before Friday.
- **Location or platform:** In person or on Zoom.
- Additional meetings will be arranged when needed before major deadlines.
- Each meeting will include progress updates, blockers, and task assignments.
- A rotating note-taker will record decisions, task owners, and deadlines in our shared meeting notes.

Members who cannot attend will notify the team at least 12 hours beforehand whenever possible. In an emergency, they will notify the team as soon as they can.

Absent members will provide a written progress update and review the meeting notes within 24 hours.

## 3. Communication

We will use Canvas and Discord for daily communication and Google Docs for meeting notes and team documents. We will use our Git repository and project board to track code changes and assigned tasks.

For urgent matters, we will use Canvas Inbox and Discord and clearly explain the issue and relevant deadline. Urgent messages do not create an expectation of overnight availability.

Important decisions made in private conversations will be summarized in the team channel so that all four members receive the same information.

## 4. Response Expectations

Members will respond to messages requiring their input within 24 hours. A response may acknowledge the message and state when a fuller answer will be available.

If a member expects to be unavailable for more than 24 hours, they will notify the team in advance when possible and explain how their tasks will be covered.

If a member repeatedly misses this expectation, a teammate will check in privately. The team will then agree on a practical communication plan. Continued problems will be handled through our accountability and conflict-resolution procedures.

## 5. Respect and Professional Conduct

We will discuss ideas and work respectfully, even when we disagree. Feedback will focus on specific issues and possible improvements rather than personal criticism.

Insults, harassment, discriminatory comments, dismissive behavior, and taking credit for another member’s work are unacceptable.

During meetings, each member will have an opportunity to share updates and opinions. We will avoid interrupting and invite input from members who have not yet spoken. Members may also provide written feedback.

## 6. Accountability

Every task will have a named owner, a deadline, and a clear description of what completion means. Members will update the project board before each regular meeting and whenever a significant blocker arises.

Our default target is to complete work at least 48 hours before the course submission deadline, allowing time for review, testing, and integration.

If someone expects to miss a deadline, they will notify the team as soon as the risk becomes clear. The team will discuss the cause and agree on support, a revised deadline, or task redistribution.

Repeated unmet commitments will be documented factually and discussed with the member. If an agreed improvement plan does not resolve the problem, we will seek guidance from the TA or instructor. Contributions will be recorded accurately.

## 7. Decision-Making

We will first seek consensus by discussing the available options, assignment requirements, and relevant evidence.

If consensus cannot be reached, all four members will have an opportunity to vote. A decision supported by at least three members will be adopted.

If the vote is tied 2–2, we will identify the main disagreement and compare the options against agreed criteria, such as correctness, complexity, time, and maintainability. For technical decisions, we may run a small experiment before discussing and voting again.

If the tie remains and blocks progress, we will ask the TA or instructor for guidance. Major decisions and their reasons will be recorded in the shared notes.

Changes to this team agreement require approval from all team members, as described in Section 11.

## 8. Conflict Resolution

We will use the following steps:

1. The members involved will discuss the issue directly and respectfully, using specific examples.
2. If it remains unresolved, the full team will meet to hear each perspective without interruption.
3. The team will agree on actions, responsibilities, and a date to check whether the solution is working.
4. If the issue continues, we will contact the TA or instructor with a factual summary of the problem and the steps already taken.

A member may seek immediate assistance for harassment, discrimination, or a situation in which they feel unsafe addressing the issue directly.

## 9. Anticipated Challenges

| Challenge | Our Response |
|-----------|--------------|
| Missed meetings | Require advance notice when possible, written updates, and review of meeting notes. |
| Missed deadlines | Raise risks early, identify blockers, and agree on support or revised assignments. |
| Unequal contributions | Review workload weekly and rebalance tasks based on effort and complexity. |
| Poor communication | Use one main team channel, follow the 24-hour response expectation, and check in when communication breaks down. |
| Scheduling conflicts | Share availability early and use written updates when an additional meeting is impractical. |
| Different technical experience | Pair on unfamiliar tasks, share explanations, and allow time for learning. |
| Disagreements | Follow the decision-making and conflict-resolution procedures. |
| Integration problems | Make small changes, request teammate review, and run tests before merging code. |
